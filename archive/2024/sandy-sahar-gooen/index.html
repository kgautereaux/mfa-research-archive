Summary

Voice type labels in musical theatre carry assumptions about range, weight and gender that the repertoire itself does not always support. This thesis analyzes tessitura in tenor and baritone roles, comparing original productions with contemporary revivals, and examines the baritenor as a category sitting between the two. It treats vocal demand as something measurable rather than assumed, and asks what casting and teaching practice inherit when a label stops matching what a role actually requires of a singer.
